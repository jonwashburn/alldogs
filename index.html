ALL DOGS is a growing collection of digital dog paintings by Wubbushi, made to be owned as NFTs. Each dog is individually composed and painted in code. You choose the dog. You give it a name.

There is a reason to choose carefully. Selling a living dog can turn it into a zombie and spread the infection to other dogs. What happens to a dog changes its painting, and stays in its record.

Why keep your dog?

Someone else's dog
may depend on it.

A sale can start a chain of infections. Keeping your dog means you won't start one. It can still catch an infection from another dog, though, and living dogs can die naturally.

The collection rules

What happens
after adoption.

Your dog starts living when you adopt it. The sale rules below apply when an owner sells a dog after adoption. The first adoption itself does not turn a dog into a zombie.

01

A sale can turn your dog into a zombie.

Selling a living dog normally turns it into a zombie. On its first resale only, the dog has a 1 in 20 chance to resist and become an immortal hero instead.

02

The infection can spread.

Each new zombie has a 50% chance to infect one other living dog, turning that dog into a zombie too. The newly infected dog gets the same chance to infect one more. The chain can stop after any dog, or keep going.

If your sale starts the chain, your X handle and the sale are permanently recorded on your dog and every dog infected by that chain. The first infected dog is generation one, the next is generation two, and so on. Every generation traces back to the original sale.

03

An immortal can rescue a dog.

Each immortal returns one infection-created zombie to living. A dog turned into a zombie by a sale cannot be rescued. Immortals also get a new background and one of five powers.

All dogs go to heaven

Living dogs
can become angels.

Every eligible living dog has a 1 in 1,600 chance of natural death each day (0.0625%). The dog becomes an angel, and you keep the artwork. Its lifespan and cause of death stay in the record.

Natural death does not spread infection. Holding on to your dog prevents a sale; it cannot guarantee a long life.

Why dogs · A note from Wubbushi

The drawings that would
have been on the fridge.

Nobody has a picture.

My other collection, HOLY, is an evangelical adolescence between 1975 and 1995: church buses, revival carpet, motel pools, baptisms, hard flash. My mom's photo albums were what she prized most. They were lost in hasty moves and storage units nobody kept paying for, and by the time I was ready to go back for them, and I mean ready in the way you have to be ready for something like that, there was nothing left to restore. Nobody has a picture of those years. Nobody photographs the part you remember anyway.

I can still paint the dogs.

ALL DOGS comes from the same childhood. I wrote a program to paint the way a kid paints: a fat outline, paint running out mid-stroke, an eye a little wrong. These are the drawings that would have been on the fridge, made now by a machine I spend my afternoons teaching.

The albums would not have brought anyone back either. You make the record anyway. I can paint the dog. Its collar records who held on to it: your handle from the first day, and whether you kept the dog or sold it.

The medium

The paint is code.

I've been building a brush in code. Each bristle carries a finite amount of paint, picks up what's still wet and leaves a mark where it touches the surface. Long strokes run dry. I write each dog's composition into the painter: its face, clothes, pose and the world around it.

Watch her take shape.

The ground is ready. Now the dog: her gown, her wig, her face, the little cake. Watch the paint travel across the canvas as the original brushwork unfolds.

Look at what the brush leaves.

Drag white through wet grey and the brush picks up grey. As it moves, the clean colour comes back. A long stroke thins into streaks. The weave shows through where the paint is thin.

How to adopt

How a dog
becomes yours.

Start with an application. You don't need to know an owner yet. You'll get a public link to share, so an eligible owner can find you and vouch for you.

01

Apply, then share your link.

Enter your X handle and Ethereum wallet address. Applying takes no payment or wallet connection. Share the application link and save your private receipt. Keep the receipt to yourself.

02

An owner vouches for you.

The owner opens your link, connects the wallet registered with their adoption and signs a message. There is no transaction or payment. Wubbushi reviews your application and checks your identity. A vouch does not guarantee acceptance.

03

Choose. Name. Bring home.

Once approved, you'll hear from Wubbushi when your turn is ready. Log into the private gallery, choose your dog and give it a name. Confirm your adoption to mint the artwork directly to your wallet. There is nothing to pay upfront.

You then have seven days from receiving your dog to pay Wubbushi what you think it is worth. The amount you pay is recorded as your valuation in the dog's attributes. After seven days, that record is permanent.

Who can vouch?

You can vouch if you own a dog, have never sold it, and none of the people you directly vouched for has ever sold their dog. If one of those people vouches for someone who later sells, that sale does not count against you.

Infection and natural death do not count as sales. A sale that creates an immortal still counts.

There is no lifetime limit to vouches, but only three can be pending at once. Each stays pending until the applicant adopts or you withdraw it.

How does the pack begin?

Wubbushi invites the founding owners. After that, eligible owners vouch for new applicants. He keeps painting and adding dogs, so the collection has no fixed final size.

ONE DIRECT ADOPTION. EVER.Each person may adopt one dog directly, ever. Selling it, a change of state or new dogs joining the collection never gives you another direct adoption. This limit does not cover secondary-market purchases.
