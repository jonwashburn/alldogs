The collection rules

What happens
after adoption.

Every newly adopted dog starts living. Your initial adoption does not turn it into a zombie. What happens next depends on keeping, reselling, infection and time.

These are the planned rules. The game is still in development; the final transfer, timing and randomness details will be published before paid adoption opens.

01

A resale can kill your dog.

Resell a living dog and it becomes a zombie, with one exception: one in twenty dogs fights off its first resale and becomes an immortal hero instead.

02

The next dog could be someone else's.

Each new zombie has a 50% chance of killing another living dog and turning it into a zombie. If your sale started it, your X handle and that sale are permanently recorded on your dog and every dog infected in the chain.

The first dog infected is generation one. It gets the same 50% chance to infect another, creating generation two, still traced back to your sale. Every new zombie gets that chance, so the chain can keep going.

03

An immortal brings one back.

Each immortal rescues one zombie turned by infection, returning it to living. A dog turned by a sale cannot be rescued. Immortals receive a new background and one of five special powers, to be announced before paid adoption.

All dogs go to heaven

Even a dog you keep
can't stay forever.

Every eligible living dog has a 1 in 1,600 chance of natural death each day (0.0625%). When it happens, the dog becomes an angel. You still own the artwork; its appearance changes, and its lifespan and cause of transition stay in the record.

Some lives are short. Some run for years. The collar remembers who was there.

Every life leaves a record

One collar.
A whole lifetime.

Each dog's collar is its permanent ownership and life record. Living, zombie, immortal or angel: the history stays with the dog.

Why dogs · A note from Wubbushi

The record of a childhood
that has no record.

Nobody has a picture.

My other collection, HOLY, is an evangelical adolescence between 1975 and 1995: church buses, revival carpet, motel pools, baptisms, hard flash. My mom's photo albums were what she prized most. They were lost in hasty moves and storage units nobody kept paying for, and by the time I was ready to go back for them, and I mean ready in the way you have to be ready for something like that, there was nothing left to restore. Nobody has a picture of those years. Nobody photographs the part you remember anyway.

So I am making the record anyway.

ALL DOGS is the same childhood in the other hand. The painter is a program I wrote to paint the way a kid paints: a fat outline, paint running out mid-stroke, an eye a little wrong. These are the drawings that would have been on the fridge, made now by a machine I spend my afternoons teaching.

A record is not a person. The albums would not have brought anyone back either. You make the record anyway. I can paint the dog. I cannot paint who held on to it. The collar records that part: your handle from the first day, and whether you kept the dog or sold it, there for good.

The medium

The paint is code.

I've been building a brush in code. Each bristle carries a finite amount of paint, picks up what's still wet and leaves a mark where it touches the surface. Long strokes run dry. I write each dog's composition into the painter: its face, clothes, pose and the world around it.

Frida, painted stroke by stroke

Thirty seconds of the brush at work. Bare cotton first, then the ground, then the dog. Each frame shows the surface after another stroke has landed.

Paint that runs out.

Drag white through wet grey and the brush picks up grey. As it moves, the clean colour comes back. A long stroke thins into streaks.

Light on the surface.

The marks build up on a canvas with tooth. Wet paint catches the light; the weave shows through where the paint is thin. Every edge is where the paint stopped.

How to adopt

Find someone
who'll vouch for you.

You can apply before you know an owner. The public Dog Pound is a preview of the art; choosing your dog happens later, in a private viewing after payment.

01

Apply and share.

Enter your X handle and Ethereum wallet address. You'll get a shareable application page. Post it to find an eligible dog owner who will vouch for you.

02

Get vouched for.

An eligible owner vouches through your application page. Wubbushi reviews your application and checks your identity. A vouch puts you forward; it doesn't guarantee an adoption.

03

Meet your dog.

When your turn is ready, Wubbushi will message you. After you pay through the official adoption flow, enter your private viewing, choose your dog and give them a name.

Who can vouch?

An owner who has never sold their dog, and whose direct invitees have never sold theirs. They can vouch for unlimited people over time, with at most three outstanding vouches at once. A completed adoption or withdrawn vouch frees a slot.

How does the pack begin?

Wubbushi invites the first owners. From there, owners welcome new people by vouching for them. Wubbushi will keep painting and adding dogs, so the collection has no fixed final size.

ONE DIRECT ADOPTION. EVER.One person, one dog adopted directly, for life. Your allowance never resets, even as new dogs arrive, or after a sale or a change of state.

Applications are open. Paid adoption is not open yet, and no deposit is collected here. Price, payment terms and availability will be confirmed before you're asked to pay. An application is not a reservation.
